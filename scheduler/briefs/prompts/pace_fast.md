## Pace: FAST (attention-first; this video is judged on whether a viewer keeps watching)
These rules come from measuring popular explainer videos: a new image every 2-4 s, nothing held longer than ~4 s, one
recurring character, a voice that breathes every 2-3 s. They override any slower pacing above, but never the content rules.
- **New picture every 2-4 seconds.** Each beat is built from 2-4 SHOTS, one per spoken phrase. Cut or whip on the spoken
  word: swap the whole composition (new image, new angle, new layout), not just a small element. No composition stays on
  screen for more than 4 s. Aim for about 15-25 cuts per minute.
- **One recurring ORIGINAL character** (a procurement manager, invented by you: a simple, consistent design with a face,
  eyes that look at things, and reactions: surprise, worry, relief). Never a known or copyrighted character. The
  character appears in at least 60% of the beats and reacts to what the voice says. People connect with a character
  that has feelings. Give the character a name in BRIEF.md and keep its colours and shape identical in every beat.
- **A mix of image types, changing every shot:** the character in a scene; free-licence photos from `assets/`
  (only when the photo clearly shows what the narration says right now; a photo that does not match, or a period or
  vintage image used for a modern point, is worse than a drawn scene, so draw instead); simple diagrams and maps; one-word kinetic
  type on the key word. Never two shots in a row of the same type.
- **Every cut lands with motion in its first 0.3 s** (punch-in, whip, pop with overshoot, a counter or line drawing on).
  A held shot still drifts, pulses or parallaxes.
- **A pattern break about every 15 s**: a zoom to the character's face, a colour flip, a fast three-shot burst, or the
  character looking at the camera. This resets attention.
- **Backgrounds must vary.** A different setting every beat or two: an office, a window onto a port or field, a plant
  floor, a plain colour field, a photo full-bleed. Alternate light and dark backgrounds. Never the same gradient,
  colour family or starburst for more than two beats in a row, and do not default to dark navy, purple or teal.
  At least four clearly different colour families across the video.
- **One running visual gag** tied to the story (for example the character's mug of tea going cold), shown 3-4 times.
- **Safety:** no flashing faster than 3 times a second and no full-screen strobes.
- **Captions:** the key word of each phrase is highlighted as it is spoken; captions stay in the bottom band.
Verification step 0 gets one more check: sample a frame every 0.5 s and confirm that no two consecutive 4-second
windows show the same composition.
